Supplier invoice exceptions: fix one ERP setting now, then pilot an agent on control and cash

Kestrow Grocers Ltd (fictional) · Finance transformation steerco, · Draft v0.3, , for Transformation Strategist review

Decision: approve a six-week agent pilot on the daily queue of price, quantity and missing-receipt exceptions for the top 40 ambient suppliers ( of invoice volume).

Recommendation: yes, in two moves.

  1. Now, with no pilot: set the ERP match tolerance to the written procedure (2% and £25 a line; the ERP is at 0%). That removes about of exceptions: a year and clerk hours.
  2. Then pilot the agent on what is left, judged on control and cash: no price above tolerance is paid without a buyer's decision on record. Released clerk time is a by-product, not the case.

What would change the recommendation

  1. K1, cash. If more than half the £ value of the unapproved overrides proves correctly priced (an agreed cost change not loaded to the PO) or was credited later, exposure falls below and the case rests on control, capacity and cycle time. Source: buyer price files, supplier statements. By .
  2. K2, technical. If the ERP cannot take match-status write-back through an approved interface, scope drops to evidence packs only. Source: engineer's interface review. By .
  3. K3, accuracy. If in shadow mode (weeks 1 and 2) the agent matches the AP lead's ruling on fewer than 95% of rule cases, it does not go live. Source: shadow log.
  4. K4, volume. If fewer than 35% of in-scope exceptions in the cohort are rule cases (the sample implies ), stop at week 3. Source: agent log.

Asks of the steerco

  1. Instruct IT to set the ERP tolerance to procedure v3.2 before go-live, once the reason for the 0% setting is confirmed (Q1).
  2. Approve the pilot scope: top 40 ambient suppliers, three exception classes, six weeks.
  3. Name one buying-team owner and agree a two-working-day reply on price queries for the cohort.
  4. Approve read access to the AP inbox, supplier portal and ERP, plus match-status write-back if K2 clears.
  5. Lock the baseline: the same cohort over the eight weeks before go-live, within-tolerance cases excluded. Success is judged on weeks 3 to 6.

        The one process to start with, and how the pilot is judged

        Scope

        The daily queue of price-variance, short-quantity and missing-receipt exceptions for the top 40 ambient suppliers.

        About in-scope exceptions across six weeks ( of volume × exception rate × in the three classes after the tolerance fix × 6/52). Assumption the cohort matches the estate-wide rate and mix; A6 re-measures it. Excluded: new suppliers, bank-detail changes, duplicates and missing PO references.

        Primary metric: control

        Share of outside-tolerance price invoices paid with a buyer's decision on record.

        Target 100%, with zero breaches. Baseline in the sample ( of ).

        Cash measure

        £ rejected or credited after a buyer's decision, by week.

        No target: this is the measured test of the estimate. Baseline: not tracked today.

        Cycle time

        Median days to clear an in-scope exception, on exceptions raised in weeks 3 and 4 so each has time to close.

        Baseline days estate-wide, re-measured on the cohort (A6). Proposed target ≤ 12 days, for the steerco to agree; it depends on the two-day buyer reply.

        Balancing measure

        Share of in-scope exception invoices paid after supplier terms must not rise.

        Baseline estate-wide, re-measured on the cohort. Holding an invoice for a buyer must not turn into paying the supplier late.

        Volume gate

        Share of in-scope exceptions the agent closes within the rules, with no reversal found in a 10% audit sample.

        Expect ≥ 45% in weeks 3 to 6; the sample implies after the tolerance fix ( before it). Stop below 35% (K4).
